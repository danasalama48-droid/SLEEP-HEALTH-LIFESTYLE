01 — The stress line

As stress climbs, sleep quality falls in almost a straight line.

Rated on an 8-point stress scale, every single step up tracks with a measurable drop in self-reported sleep quality — from 8.97 at the lowest stress level down to 5.86 at the highest. It's one of the cleanest relationships in the whole dataset.

Reading it: stress level (1–8, self-reported) plotted against average sleep quality (1–10) for everyone at that stress level. The line only moves one direction.
02 — The occupation gap

Your job title is a decent guess at your sleep schedule.

Averaging across each occupation with a meaningful sample size, engineers sleep nearly 1.6 hours more per night than salespeople — and report the lowest stress of any group in the dataset.

Reading it: bar length is average nightly sleep duration; the number at the end of each bar is that group's average stress level (1–8). Lower stress consistently sits with longer sleep.
03 — The weight connection

Sleep disorders aren't evenly distributed — they cluster hard around BMI.

Among people at a normal weight, 93% report no sleep disorder at all. That protection erodes fast: by the time someone is classified obese, a disorder is more likely than not.

04 — Two different disorders, two different jobs

Nurses and salespeople are both sleep-troubled — just not in the same way.

83% of nurses with a disorder have Sleep Apnea — a physical, breathing-related condition. Salespeople and teachers skew almost entirely toward Insomnia — a stress-linked, cognitive one. Same 40%-ish disorder rate, opposite root cause.

Sleep Apnea shows up where physical strain and irregular shifts dominate (nursing). Insomnia shows up where the job is high-pressure and cognitively loaded (sales, teaching). The dataset can't prove causation — but the split is too clean to be noise.

05 — A smaller, steadier gap

Women in this data sleep a little more, and report noticeably less stress.

The gender gap here is modest on sleep duration but wider on stress: women average 4.68 out of 8, men average 6.08 out of 8 — a gap almost as large as the entire range between the calmest and most stressed occupations.
